The problem

Nobody told the monitors.

Earth displays emit essentially nothing above 400 nm. It does not matter what is on the screen. To a Korveth it is a slab of warm grey nothing, and has been since onboarding.

  • ▥

    Screens emit no UV

    Not a contrast problem. Not a font-size problem. The photons your Korveth staff need are simply not being produced by the hardware.

  • ▤

    Print is barely better

    Toner and paper do have UV behaviour, but nothing on an Earth memo was ever designed around it. Legibility is accidental at best.

  • ▦

    So everything gets read aloud

    Which is how a dock rota becomes a two-hour meeting, and how “bay 3” becomes “bay 8” twice in one shift.

The product

One problem, two halves.

Something has to emit the ultraviolet, and something has to decide what to put in it. We sell both, and they are considerably better together.

How it works

Three samples in, three bands out.

  1. 01

    Read what the camera caught

    An RGB image samples a scene at roughly 600, 550 and 450 nm. That is all anyone has to work with, and we are honest about it.

  2. 02

    Fit the curve, slide it down

    We model reflectance as a quadratic through those three points and read it off where a Korveth eye would look instead.

  3. 03

    Ship one small matrix

    Every step is linear, so the whole translation collapses to a 3×3 matrix. We send that, and the panel does the rest.

The matrix is on screen in the demo above, under Diagnostics. Each row sums to 1, so neutral greys stay neutral no matter how far you push the slider.

Talk to us

The honest bit

Questions we get asked in the lobby.

Is this real ultraviolet?

The panel emits real ultraviolet. The demo does not — your screen cannot. What you see in the browser is a false-colour preview of the same band data the panel would be driven with.

Can you recover UV from an ordinary photo?

No, and neither can anyone else. A human camera records no ultraviolet, and nobody can invent data that was never captured. Any startup telling you otherwise is lying to you at a valuation.

What we do is re-encode the information that is in the image into bands a Korveth can reach. For the actual job that is enough, because the problem was never missing UV detail — it was a monitor emitting none at all.

Will my Earth staff be able to read the panel?

Not really, which several managers have described as the best part. The shade in the box is for humans who walk past and feel strange about it.

What happens to the images I drop into the demo?

Nothing. There is no server, no upload and no tracking on this page. Every pixel is processed locally in your browser.

Where does the camera feed go?

Nowhere. Frames are pulled straight into a canvas, translated and drawn back out, all inside the tab. Nothing is recorded and nothing is transmitted. Stop the camera and the browser drops the stream.

Your browser will ask permission first, and the page needs a secure origin — it works here over https, and on localhost, but not from a file:// path.
