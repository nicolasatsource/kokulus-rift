How it works

Three samples in, three bands out.

  1. Step 01

    Read what the camera caught

    An RGB image samples a scene at roughly 600, 550 and 450 nm. That is the whole input.

  2. Step 02

    Fit the curve, slide it down

    Reflectance is modelled as a quadratic through those three points, then read off where the Korvethan receptors sit.

  3. Step 03

    Ship one small matrix

    Every step is linear, so the translation collapses to a 3×3 matrix. That is the Spectral Interchange Format.

The matrix is on screen in the demo above, under Diagnostics. Each row sums to 1, so neutral greys stay neutral no matter how far you push it.

Questions

Asked in the lobby.

Is this real ultraviolet?

The device emits real ultraviolet, tight-beam and contained by the geometry of the chassis. The demo does not — your screen cannot. What the browser shows is a false-colour preview of the same band data the emitter array would be driven with.

Can you recover UV from an ordinary photo?

No. A human camera records no ultraviolet, and nothing can recover what was never captured. The encoder re-expresses the information that is in the image into bands the array can drive.

For the actual job that is the right operation, because the problem was never missing UV detail. It was a monitor emitting none at all.

Is the UV output safe for everyone at the desk?

The UV array is monitor-facing and tight-beam, contained by the geometry of the chassis. The ambient emitter is full-spectrum across a 3-foot radius. Dawn mode runs at 10,000 lux for human wellness, and Host mode runs a 45-minute guest course from the same hardware.

What happens to the images I drop into the demo?

Nothing. There is no server, no upload and no tracking on this page. Every pixel is processed locally in your browser.

Where does the camera feed go?

Nowhere. Frames are pulled into a canvas, translated and drawn back out, all inside the tab. Nothing is recorded and nothing is transmitted. Stop the camera and the browser drops the stream.

Your browser will ask permission first, and the page needs a secure origin — it works here over https, and on localhost, but not from a file:// path.
